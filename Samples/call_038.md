Member: So I'll lose my current coverage?
Agent Michael: Moving out of your plan's service area is a qualifying life event that allows you to either enroll in your new employer's

Member: Yes, they do. It starts after a 90-day waiting period.
Agent Michael: That's an important gap. You'll have a 90-day period where you need to actively secure coverage. Options are: COBRA

health insurance if cost is a priority, though that comes with significant coverage limitations.
Member: What's the deadline to make these decisions?
Agent Michael: For COBRA: your employer is required to send you a COBRA election notice within 14 days of your termination. You'll
then have 60 days to elect coverage. If you elect it, the coverage is retroactive to your coverage end date, which means if something
happens in that window, you can elect COBRA and have coverage applied. For ACA marketplace: you have 60 days from your qualifying
life event — your move — to enroll in a Texas plan.
Member: Which option is usually better — COBRA or marketplace?
Agent Michael: It depends on income. If your income is between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level, you may qualify for ACA
marketplace subsidies that make marketplace coverage significantly cheaper than COBRA. If you're above that threshold, COBRA
continuity of care may be worth the higher premium, especially if you have ongoing treatment. I'd recommend getting a marketplace
quote at healthcare.gov as soon as you have your Texas address to compare.
Member: What happens to my current in-network doctors?
Agent Michael: Your California-based doctors won't be in-network in a Texas plan, so you'll be starting fresh with your provider
relationships in Texas. Any ongoing care — prescriptions, specialist relationships — should be coordinated before you leave. Ask your
California doctors for medical records and prescription histories so your new Texas providers can pick up seamlessly.
Member: This is a lot more complicated than I thought. Can you send me a summary of what we discussed?
Agent Michael: I'll note everything on your account and I'm giving you a case number: RL-2026-44891. I'll also email you a relocation
guide with next-step timelines. The single most important action right now: check with your new employer's HR about the exact start date
of your new coverage — that determines how long your coverage gap actually is.
Member: RL-2026-44891. Thank you, Michael. I came in thinking this would be simple.
Agent Michael: Moves always have more moving parts than expected. You've got time to plan this right.
