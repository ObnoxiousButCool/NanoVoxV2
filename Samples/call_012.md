Agent Linda: Choice Administrators member services, this is Linda.

am a 61-year-old man with Type 2 diabetes. My endocrinologist prescribed a CGM because my sugar levels have been unstable and
I've had two hypoglycemic episodes this year where I didn't even feel them coming. Without a CGM I am flying blind. How is that not

Agent Linda: Raymond, I'm pulling up your account and the denial right now. And I want to say — two hypoglycemic episodes without

says.
Member: Please. Because the letter I got said 'does not meet clinical criteria' and gave me a phone number that has been useless to
call.
Agent Linda: Okay, I have the denial. The reason listed is that the clinical criteria for CGM coverage on your plan requires
documentation of 'hypoglycemia unawareness' specifically tied to insulin therapy, and the documentation submitted did not reference
insulin use. Are you currently on insulin?
Member: I was started on insulin three months ago. Basal insulin, one injection at night. My endocrinologist's notes should show that.
Agent Linda: If you're on insulin and have documented hypoglycemia unawareness, you should meet the clinical criteria — the denial
may have been based on incomplete or ambiguous documentation rather than a clinical decision against you. This is fixable through
appeal. Your endocrinologist needs to submit documentation that specifically states: one, current insulin use; two, documented
hypoglycemia unawareness — those two episodes you mentioned; and three, clinical justification for CGM over traditional finger-stick
monitoring.
Member: My doctor is going to be furious. She already spent time on that prior auth. To have to do it again because of a
documentation issue...
Agent Linda: I hear you. And I want to offer something that might help — I can give you and your doctor's office the exact clinical criteria
language so the appeal is written to directly address what the reviewer is looking for. That minimizes back-and-forth.
Member: Yes, please. What's the language?
Agent Linda: The clinical criteria for CGM coverage states: member must be on insulin therapy, and provider must document either
hypoglycemia unawareness or HbA1c not at goal despite optimal management. Given you're on insulin and had two unawareness
episodes, your doctor should state both. The appeal documentation should also include your most recent HbA1c result and the dates of
the hypoglycemic episodes.
Member: Okay, I'm writing all of this down. What's the deadline to file the appeal?
Agent Linda: Appeals must be filed within 180 days of the denial date. Your denial was issued August 1st, so you have until the end of
January. But given the clinical urgency, I'd suggest filing as soon as your doctor can put the documentation together — ideally this week.
Member: And if it's denied again?
Agent Linda: A second denial can be appealed to an independent external reviewer — a physician outside our network. External
appeals for DME related to insulin management have a meaningful reversal rate. You can also request expedited review if your doctor
documents urgent clinical need.
Member: Thank you, Linda. This is the first time someone has actually told me what to do next rather than just reading me the denial
letter back.
Agent Linda: I'm glad. You shouldn't have to fight this hard for something your doctor says you need. Good luck, Raymond.
