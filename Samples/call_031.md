## Transcript

Agent Linda: Choice Administrators member services, this is Linda.
Member: Linda, Victor Huang, member ID CHM-7702889. I take Synthroid for my thyroid — I've been on it for eight years. This year
my copay went from $45 to $120 a month. Nobody told me it was changing. I take this every single day. $120 a month, every month,
for the rest of my life is not something I can just absorb. What happened and what can I do about it?
Agent Linda: Victor, I understand. A jump from $45 to $120 on a medication you take daily and indefinitely is a significant financial
change and you deserve a clear explanation. Let me look at your formulary and this medication.
Member: Please.
Agent Linda: Synthroid — levothyroxine brand name — was reclassified on January 1st from Tier 2 to Tier 3 on your plan's formulary.
Tier 3 is our non-preferred brand tier, which has a higher copay. This reclassification happens when the plan's pharmacy benefit
manager updates the formulary based on cost negotiations and clinical equivalence data.
Member: Why would a medication I've been stable on for eight years suddenly be non-preferred?
Agent Linda: The reclassification is typically based on cost — a generic equivalent is now well-established. Generic levothyroxine is
therapeutically equivalent to Synthroid for most patients and is on our Tier 1 formulary at a $20 copay. The plan is steering members
toward the generic to reduce costs.
Member: But my doctor specifically prescribes Synthroid because some thyroid patients absorb the generic differently. He told me
that.
Agent Linda: That's a real and documented clinical consideration — thyroid patients can have absorption variability between brand and
generic formulations. If your physician has a documented clinical reason for prescribing Synthroid specifically over generic, you can
request a formulary exception. Your doctor submits a letter documenting the clinical necessity of brand Synthroid, and if approved, the
medication is covered at Tier 2 rates — your previous $45 copay.
Member: How long does the exception process take?
Agent Linda: Formulary exception reviews typically take 72 hours to 5 business days. Exceptions for brand thyroid medications based
on documented clinical necessity are approved at a meaningful rate. I'd also ask your doctor — some physicians write 'brand medically
necessary, do not substitute' on the prescription, which supports the exception.
Member: I'm going to call his office today. What should I tell them to submit?
Agent Linda: Have them submit a formulary exception request — they can do it via fax to 1-800-555-7200 or through the provider portal.
The letter should reference your specific Synthroid stability over 8 years and the clinical basis for avoiding generic substitution. They
should also include the Dispense As Written notation.
Member: Okay. In the meantime I'm going to have to pay $120 again next month if this isn't resolved. Can I do anything about the
months I've already paid at $120?
Agent Linda: If the exception is approved, it can be backdated to the start of the plan year for cost adjustment purposes in some cases
— but I can't guarantee that without reviewing the exception outcome. It's worth raising with the exception reviewer when it's submitted.
Your case number for tracking is FE-2026-55210.
Member: FE-2026-55210. Okay. Thank you, Linda. This is frustrating but I understand what happened and what to do.
Agent Linda: That's exactly what I wanted. Good luck with the exception — I think you have a strong case.
