Agent Linda: Choice Administrators member services, this is Linda.
Member: Hi Linda, my name is Helen Park, member ID CHM-7741009. I'm calling about my son Marcus — he's turning 26 in October
and I know that's when he can no longer be on my insurance. I want to make sure he transitions without any coverage gap. Can you
walk me through what he needs to do?
Agent Linda: Helen, you're calling at exactly the right time — two months ahead gives Marcus good lead time. You're correct that
dependent children age off the plan at age 26. Marcus's coverage will end on the last day of the month in which he turns 26. What is his
birthday in October?
Member: October 15th.
Agent Linda: So his coverage under your plan ends October 31st. That gives him all of November to start a new plan. Actually — turning
26 and aging off a parent's plan is a qualifying life event, which gives Marcus a 60-day special enrollment period on the ACA marketplace
or through his own employer's plan. He can enroll in coverage effective November 1st if he acts within that window.
Member: He has a job but I don't think they offer insurance. What does he do then?
Agent Linda: The ACA marketplace is his best option then. He should go to healthcare.gov and shop for individual plans. If his income is
under about $54,000 for a single person, he may qualify for subsidies that meaningfully reduce the monthly premium. He can start
shopping now to see what options look like, though he won't be able to officially enroll until his qualifying event occurs — either turning 26
or coverage ending.
Member: Can he stay on my plan a few extra months?
Agent Linda: Unfortunately no — the age 26 cutoff is set by federal law and plans cannot continue coverage past the last day of the
month of his 26th birthday. COBRA is technically an option — we'd be required to offer him a COBRA election notice — but for a
26-year-old, marketplace plans are almost always more affordable than COBRA, which is the full premium cost with no employer
contribution.
Member: What do we need to do on our end?
Agent Linda: You don't need to do anything to initiate the removal — it happens automatically on October 31st based on Marcus's date
of birth in our system. We'll send him a COBRA election notice automatically. He should also receive a notice about his marketplace SEP
rights. The main action item is his: shop on healthcare.gov before October 31st so he's ready to enroll immediately after.
Member: Is there anything he should do to prepare? Like medical records or prescriptions?
Agent Linda: That's a thoughtful question. He should request a 90-day supply of any ongoing prescriptions before the coverage ends —
under your plan, that would be the mail-order benefit. He should also get any outstanding care done while he's still covered — dental,
vision, any pending referrals. And ask his doctors if they'll accept his new insurance before the transition — most will, but it's worth
confirming.
Member: This is really comprehensive. I feel much better. Should Marcus call you directly?
Agent Linda: He can, or you can share this information with him. The most important number: healthcare.gov. The most important date:
October 31st. He has until December 29th — 60 days from the qualifying event — to enroll in a marketplace plan with coverage effective
November 1st.
Member: Thank you, Linda. Very thorough.
