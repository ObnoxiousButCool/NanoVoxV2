Member: Okay. Tell me about that process because my physical therapist mentioned something about an extended authorization but
she wasn't sure exactly how to submit it.
Agent Carlos: Your PT's office needs to submit a request for continued care authorization. It needs to include a progress note

necessary to prevent re-injury or achieve functional restoration. For post-surgical PT, extended authorization requests have a reasonably
good approval rate — especially for rotator cuff surgeries where 30 visits is often insufficient.
Member: That's good to hear. How many additional visits can they request?
Agent Carlos: There's no fixed number — they can request what they clinically justify. Most requests for post-surgical cases are for 10 to
20 additional visits. If your PT documents that you're still below functional thresholds, that's the argument.
Member: And how long does the authorization review take? I have a visit scheduled in two weeks and I'm going to hit my limit before
then.
Agent Carlos: Standard review is 5 to 7 business days. If you submit in the next few days, you should have a decision before you run
out. I'm going to note on your account that this is a post-surgical case approaching limit, which flags it for PT utilization reviewers. I'd also
recommend your PT's office call our prior auth line directly — 1-800-555-0310 — and reference your post-surgical status in the first call to
make sure it gets routed to the right reviewers.
Member: What if it gets denied?
Agent Carlos: You have the right to appeal within 60 days of a denial. For musculoskeletal post-surgical denials, appeals are often
successful with additional clinical documentation, particularly a functional assessment like a shoulder ROM measurement that shows
you're below normal thresholds. The appeal would go to an independent reviewer, not the same team that denied it.
Member: I appreciate all of that. I'm a little frustrated that 30 visits was set as a limit when it's clearly not enough for major shoulder
surgery, but I understand that's a plan design thing and not something you can change right now.
Agent Carlos: You're right that I can't change the plan structure, and your frustration is valid — 30 visits for a post-surgical recovery is a
common pain point. What I can do is note your feedback formally, and I'd encourage you to submit a written member grievance as well —
those are taken seriously in plan design reviews. Is there anything else I can help with today?
