## Transcript

Agent Danielle: Choice Administrators member services, this is Danielle.
Member: Hi, Jason Kim, member ID CHM-8805567. I'm on the CalChoice High Deductible Health Plan. I've heard I can open an HSA
— a Health Savings Account — alongside it. Is that true, and does my plan actually qualify?
Member: My broker, Patricia Nunez, explained the deductible trade-off properly when she put me on this plan, and she mentioned an
HSA might pair with it. I just never followed up on the HSA part.
Agent Danielle: Jason, yes — you're correct, and your plan does qualify. The CalChoice HDHP is an IRS-qualified High Deductible
Health Plan, which means you are eligible to open and contribute to a Health Savings Account. Let me tell you how it works.
Member: Please. I've been meaning to look into this for months.
Agent Danielle: An HSA is a tax-advantaged savings account that works alongside your HDHP. The triple tax benefit is what makes it
powerful: contributions are tax-deductible, the money grows tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are also tax-free.
No other account offers all three of those benefits simultaneously.
Member: That sounds almost too good. What's the catch?
Agent Danielle: The catch is that you can only use HSA funds for qualified medical expenses — doctor visits, prescriptions, dental,
vision, medical equipment. If you withdraw for non-medical expenses before age 65, you pay income tax plus a 20% penalty. After 65, it
behaves like a traditional retirement account — withdraw for anything, just pay income tax on non-medical withdrawals.
Member: So if I don't use it all, it rolls over and keeps growing?
Agent Danielle: Exactly. Unlike FSAs, HSA funds never expire. They roll over year after year. Many people use their HSA as a
supplemental retirement account for healthcare costs — which tend to be the largest expense in retirement.
Member: How much can I contribute this year?
Agent Danielle: For 2026, the IRS limit for an individual HSA is $4,150. If you're on a family plan, it's $8,300. If you're 55 or older, you
can contribute an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution.
Member: I'm 33, single — so $4,150. Can I contribute the full amount even if I didn't have the account for the full year?
Agent Danielle: If you've been on an HSA-eligible HDHP since January 1st, yes — you can contribute the full $4,150 for the year. If you
enrolled mid-year, there's a prorated calculation, but there's also a last-month rule that may allow full-year contribution in some
circumstances.
Member: I enrolled January 1st this year. So I can contribute the full amount before December 31st?
Agent Danielle: Yes. You can open an HSA at most banks or credit unions — it's a separate account you open yourself, not through us.
Fidelity, Lively, and HealthEquity are commonly used HSA providers. You'd fund it directly and list your medical expenses when you use
it.
Member: This is fantastic. Why didn't I know about this sooner?
Agent Danielle: It's one of the most underutilized benefits in the US. Is there anything else I can clarify?
Member: No, that covers it. Thank you, Danielle. I'm opening one today.
