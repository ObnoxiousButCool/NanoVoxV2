## Transcript

Agent Linda: Choice Administrators member services, this is Linda.
Member: Hi, Linda. Carlos Rivera, CHM-3305671. I have insurance through my employer — Choice Administrators — and my wife's
employer covers me too. I've been getting bills from providers and I don't know how to handle them because I'm not sure which
insurance is supposed to pay first. One doctor billed me saying I owe the full amount, another one is asking for my secondary
insurance information. I'm confused about how all of this works.
Agent Linda: Carlos, dual coverage situations are confusing and you're right to call. Let me explain how coordination of benefits works
so you can handle these situations on your own going forward. First — do you know which plan was set up as primary and which as
secondary?
Member: I don't. I filled out a form when I enrolled here that asked about other coverage, but I didn't think much about it at the time.
Agent Linda: Most insurers use what's called the birthday rule to determine which plan is primary for a member who is covered by two
plans as an employee-level member rather than a dependent. The plan of the employer whose policyholder has the earlier birthday in the
calendar year — not the year, just the month and day — is typically primary. So if your birthday is in March and your wife's is in
September, your plan through your employer would be primary for you.
Member: My birthday is in May. My wife's is in February. So her plan would be primary for me?
Agent Linda: Under the birthday rule, yes — her plan would be primary for you because February comes before May. However, I want
to check what's actually on file here. Let me look at your COB designation. Okay, I can see that your account shows your wife's plan as
primary for you. But I see that when a claim came in last month, it was submitted to us first — that's why the EOB shows us paying as if
we were primary, and that's likely causing the confusion with the provider.
Member: So the doctor billed you first when they should have billed my wife's insurance first?
Agent Linda: Exactly. When two insurances are involved, the provider should bill the primary insurer first, then submit the remaining
balance to the secondary. You shouldn't owe anything — or very little — because the secondary insurance usually covers what the
primary didn't. The problem is if the provider billed us first, we processed it as primary and may have applied your deductible and
coinsurance, leaving you with a balance that the second insurance would have covered.
Member: So what do I do about the bill I got?
Agent Linda: You need to tell the provider to submit the claim to your wife's plan first — they are the primary. Once that processes, give
the provider your Choice Administrators information as secondary. The secondary will then cover most or all of the remaining member
responsibility. You should end up with little to no out-of-pocket.
Member: And the claim that already went through you as primary — does that need to be reprocessed?
Agent Linda: Yes, and that happens automatically once the primary processes the claim and sends an explanation to the secondary.
When the primary sends us the coordination notice, we reprocess our payment to reflect our correct secondary role. The net effect is that
your cost goes down significantly.
Member: This is actually making sense now. I'm going to call my wife's insurance today. Is there anything I should tell them?
Agent Linda: Give them the claim details and let them know you have secondary coverage through Choice Administrators. Ask them to
process the claim and send us a coordination notice. Our payer ID for secondary COB submissions is CHC-4490.
Member: CHC-4490. Got it. Thank you, Linda. This was like untangling a knot.
Agent Linda: That's a perfect description of dual coverage. You've got it now. Good luck, Carlos.
