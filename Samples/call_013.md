## Transcript

Agent Sarah: Choice Administrators member services, this is Sarah. How can I help?
Member: Hi, my name is Sophie Martinez, member ID CHM-9021445. I got this letter in the mail that says Explanation of Benefits and
I don't know what it is or what I'm supposed to do with it. My mom said I should call you. Am I in trouble? Do I owe money?
Member: I've never had my own insurance before. My broker, Anthony Salerno, signed me up but he never explained any of the
paperwork I'd be getting. Am I in trouble? Do I owe money?
Agent Sarah: Sophie, don't worry — an EOB is not a bill. You are not in trouble. An Explanation of Benefits is just a summary we send
you after a claim is processed so you can see how the cost was divided between us and you. Do you have the EOB in front of you?
Member: Yes. It has a lot of columns and I don't know what any of them mean.
Agent Sarah: Let's go through it together. The first column you'll usually see is the 'Billed Amount' — that's what your provider charged
us, before any adjustments. What number do you see there?
Member: $850.
Agent Sarah: Okay. Now look for 'Plan Discount' or 'Negotiated Adjustment' — that's the amount your provider agreed to accept less of
because they're in-network with us. What does that show?
Member: It says $312.
Agent Sarah: Good. So the actual cost after the discount is $850 minus $312, which is $538. That's called the 'Allowed Amount.' Now
look for what the plan paid — usually labeled 'Plan Paid' or 'Insurance Paid.'
Member: It says $488.
Agent Sarah: And the last column should show 'Member Responsibility' or 'Your Share.' What's that number?
Member: $50. So I owe $50?
Agent Sarah: Yes — $50 is your responsibility, which is your copay for this type of service. The EOB is confirming that amount. But
again — this is not the bill. Your provider will send you a separate bill for that $50. The EOB is just showing you how the math worked.
Member: Oh! That makes so much more sense. So I should wait for a bill from the doctor, not pay this paper?
Agent Sarah: Exactly right. The EOB is for your records — file it or save it digitally. The actual bill will come from the provider. If you ever
get a bill that's higher than what the EOB shows as your responsibility, call us, because the provider can only charge you what the EOB
says is your member responsibility — not more.
Member: That's really good to know. Nobody ever explained that to me. Is there anything else on the EOB I should look at?
Agent Sarah: Yes — check the date of service and the provider name to make sure it matches a visit you actually had. EOB fraud, while
uncommon, can happen if someone used your insurance without your knowledge. If anything doesn't look right, call us.
Member: Okay. It matches — it's from my ER visit last month. Alright. I feel so much better. Thank you for walking me through this.
Agent Sarah: Of course. EOBs confuse a lot of people their first time. You did the right thing by calling.
