Agent Tony: Choice Administrators, this is Tony.
Member: Hi Tony. Priyanka Shah, CHM-7708823. I went to urgent care on August 25th and the claim was denied saying I had no
coverage on that date. But my coverage started August 1st.
Agent Tony: Let me check your effective date... I'm showing September 1st, not August 1st.
Member: That's not what I was told. My broker, Patricia Nunez, said August 1st. I have an email from her saying 'your coverage
begins August 1st.'
Agent Tony: Let me look at the enrollment timeline. Your enrollment was submitted July 22nd. Your employer's waiting period is 30 days
from hire, and you started July 15th — so 30 days from July 15th is August 14th, and coverage begins the first of the following month,
which is September 1st.
Member: So the broker calculated it as 30 days from my hire date landing in August, and assumed August 1st?
Agent Tony: That seems likely. It's a common miscalculation — people forget the 'first of the following month' step.
Member: So I have a bill from urgent care for a date I thought I was covered.
Agent Tony: Unfortunately yes. August 25th falls before your effective date, so that claim can't be covered.
Member: That's frustrating. I acted on what I was told in writing.
Agent Tony: I understand. Let me note the broker communication issue on your account. I'd also suggest you contact the urgent care
and ask for their self-pay rate — most facilities have a discounted cash rate that's significantly lower than the billed amount, and they'll
often apply it retroactively if you explain the situation.
Member: That's a good idea, I didn't know that was an option.
Agent Tony: It usually saves 30 to 50 percent. Ask specifically for the self-pay or prompt-pay discount.
Member: Okay. And the broker thing?
Agent Tony: Noted on your account. It's a communication error rather than anything serious, but it's logged.
Member: Fine. Thanks for explaining it clearly at least.
