# NanoVox — Product Architecture

**Document status:** Draft for review
**Date:** 2026-09-07
**Author:** Chief Architect
**Scope:** The **proof of concept** as a set of product capabilities — what it is made
of, who each part serves, and which parts exist today. The architecture is a product
architecture; the build behind it is a POC, and §7 says exactly how far it goes
**Diagrams:** `13-product-architecture-diagram.svg` (what the product is) and
`14-product-context-diagram.svg` (what it sits inside), generated by
`scripts/make_product_diagram.py` and `scripts/make_context_diagram.py`

---

## 1. What this document is, and what it is not

Three architecture documents now sit in this folder and they answer three different
questions. Reaching for the wrong one is the usual reason an architecture conversation
goes badly.

| Document | Question it answers | Reader |
|---|---|---|
| `10-realtime-agent-assist-architecture.md` | *What runs where, how fast, at what cost* | Engineering, infrastructure, security review |
| `11-management-pitch.md` | *Why should we fund this* | Senior management, in the room |
| **This document** | *What is the product made of, who does each part serve, and what is actually built* | Product, client-facing conversations, roadmap planning |

This document deliberately contains **no** stack, no latency budget, no deployment
topology and no cost model. All four live in document 10 and are not repeated here,
because a product architecture that drifts into infrastructure stops being usable in the
conversation it exists for.

What it does contain, and what the other two do not, is a **status per capability**. A
product architecture that draws delivered and designed capabilities identically is a
sales document, not an architecture. Every box in the diagram carries one of three
marks, and §7 counts them honestly.

## 2. The shape

Five capability bands, stacked so that **the lower a band sits, the more of the product
rests on it**. Channels enter on the left, audiences read out on the right.

```
                       ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
   CHANNELS            │  EXPERIENCE      what people touch       │      AUDIENCES
   ─────────           ├──────────────────────────────────────────┤      ─────────
   Pasted transcript   │  ACTION          what changes            │      Agent
                   ──▶ │  INTELLIGENCE    five layers             │  ──▶ Supervisor
   Recorded/live       ├──────────────────────────────────────────┤      Leadership
   Email & chat        │  FOUNDATION      what it all runs on     │
                       ├──────────────────────────────────────────┤
                       │  TRUST & GOVERNANCE   the floor          │
                       └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The ordering carries an argument, not just a layout. **Trust is drawn as the floor
rather than as a side-panel**, because every capability above it is unusable against real
member data until it holds. That is not a stylistic choice — it is the finding in §8.

## 3. Capability bands

Status vocabulary, used consistently in every table below and in the diagram:

- **LIVE** — built and running **in the POC**: demonstrable today, against a synthetic
  corpus, on localhost, without authentication (DEC-07) or redaction (DEC-08). A green
  dot is a claim about this build, never about a production deployment.
- **NEXT** — a seam exists in the code and nothing is behind it yet. Cheap to close
  because the shape is already decided.
- **LATER** — designed and costed in document 10; not started.

### 3.3 Intelligence — five layers, one model call each

This band is the product's actual content. Each layer is **one model call**, with
`max_attempts = max_retries + 2` per layer on failure.

| Layer | What it produces | Status |
|---|---|---|
| L1 · Transcription & understanding | Call type, caller, tone, duration | **LIVE** |
| L2 · Call insights | What happened, and whether it was resolved | **LIVE** |
| L3 · Agent quality | Evidence-anchored markers — the rubric does the arithmetic | **LIVE** |
| L4 · Operational BI | The systemic issue, written against a BI category with a named owner and a recommendation. **Root cause lives here**, not in L5 | **LIVE** |
| L5 · Real-time assist | What assist fired — or, more usefully, what *should* have fired and did not | **LIVE** |

Layer names are the authored ones, verbatim from the corpus panels (`Samples/call_*.md`)
and from `L4_PROMPT = "l4_operational_bi"` / `L5_PROMPT = "l5_assist"` in
`use_cases/analyze_transcript.py`. They are worth getting right in client material,
because L5 is the layer people most often mis-describe.

**L5 is the feedback loop, and it is the most under-sold capability in the product.**
`AssistOutcome.SHOULD_HAVE_FIRED` is modelled as a first-class state rather than an empty
list, so the post-call pass does not merely note that no assist appeared — it names the
rule that was missing, at the turn where it was needed, having checked the data was
available. Every `SHOULD_HAVE_FIRED` is a candidate real-time rule (doc 10 §7.1).

That matters for sequencing: **Agent Assist is LATER, but the product is already writing
its rule set.** A client who runs the post-call lane for a quarter arrives at the
real-time build with a backlog of rules derived from their own calls, rather than a blank
rules engine and a guess.

The division of labour in L3 is DEC-03 and is the single most defensible thing about the
scoring: **the model supplies evidence, the rubric supplies the number.** Same transcript
plus same rubric produces the same score every time, and every marker traces to a
transcript quote. A bare model-generated number is neither auditable nor stable across
providers.

### 3.4 Foundation — what every capability runs on

| Capability | What it provides | Status | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLM Gateway | One interface, interchangeable models — Ollama, OpenAI, Anthropic | **LIVE** | `infrastructure/llm/registry.py` |
| Scoring Rubric | Config-driven weights and thresholds | **LIVE** | `domain/scoring/`, `config/rubric.yaml` |
| Insight Store | One structured record per call — queryable, comparable, auditable | **LIVE** | `infrastructure/persistence/` |
| Knowledge Base · RAG | Approved answers, cited to a document | **LATER** | Doc 10 §8 |
| Speech to Text | Speaker-separated transcription, live and batch | **LATER** | Doc 10 §6; DEC-04 excludes it from Phase 1 |

Azure AI Foundry is registered in the gateway and **deliberately not implemented**
(DEC-05). Selecting it fails immediately with an explicit message rather than silently
falling back to another provider — a caller who asked for Azure and quietly received
OpenAI output would have no way to know their governance requirement was not met.

### 3.5 Trust & governance — the floor everything stands on

| Capability | What it guarantees | Status | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| PHI Redaction | Personal detail masked before any model call | **NEXT** | `application/ports/redaction.py` — the port is called; `NoRedaction` is behind it |
| Provider Gating | Only BAA-covered providers usable against real data | **NEXT** | `LOCAL_PROVIDERS` / `is_local()` mark the seam; no gate is enforced |
| Consent & Notice | Call-recording and AI-analysis notice, configurable per jurisdiction | **LATER** | Doc 10 §10.1 |
| Model Call Audit | Provider, model and prompt recorded per call | **LIVE** | `infrastructure/logging/llm_audit.py` |
| Data Residency | A local-only lane where nothing leaves the tenant | **LIVE** | Ollama provider; `is_local()` |
| Access Control | Role-scoped access and an access audit trail | **LATER** | DEC-07 — no authentication in Phase 1, seam documented at the API boundary |

**These six are a selection, not the control set.** Doc 10 §10.1 specifies fifteen control
areas; the nine not drawn here — data classification, encryption in transit and at rest,
retention, tenant isolation, egress allow-listing, prompt injection, DLP, and the full
audit scope — are all LATER, and drawing nine more grey boxes would make the product's
most scrutinised area look emptier than it is without adding a decision anyone can act on.
The band carries a note saying so, and this paragraph is the same undertaking in prose.
Two of them deserve naming even so:

- **Model Call Audit is narrower than it sounds.** `llm_audit.py` records provider, model
  and prompt per call. Doc 10's audit control is far wider — every PHI access with its
  purpose, every assist card shown, every export, hash-chained and retained seven years.
  The tile is honest about what exists; a reader may hear the larger claim.
- **Prompt injection is the control most specific to this product.** Transcript and email
  content is data, never instruction. Email is the sharpest vector, because an attacker
  can post arbitrary text into the pipeline. Doc 10 §10.1 carries the enforcement.

**Consent & Notice earns its place in the band despite being LATER**, because it is the
only missing control that is member-facing and can stop a go-live outright rather than be
remediated afterwards. Recording and AI-analysis notice has to be configurable per
jurisdiction — two-party-consent states differ — and a member who declines analysis must
be flagged and excluded. Worth establishing early which states Choice Administrators'
book actually covers; that answer changes when this becomes urgent, not whether.

## 4. Channels

| Channel | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pasted transcript | **LIVE** | DEC-04 — the only ingestion path in Phase 1 |
| Recorded & live calls | **LATER** | Blocked on speech-to-text, not on the pipeline |
| Email & chat | **LATER** | Already text — joins the same pipeline at redaction |

The domain model is channel-agnostic on purpose. Email and chat are **not** a second
product; they are already text, so they skip transcription and enter at the same seam
everything else does. That is the cheapest expansion available and it is worth saying out
loud in a client conversation.

## 5. Audiences

| Audience | What they get today | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Agent | Live guidance while the call is happening | **LATER** |
| Supervisor | Every call scored, not a sample; live alerting later | **LIVE** |
| Leadership | Trends, topics and agent quality — one set of numbers | **LIVE** |

The supervisor row carries the strongest commercial line in the document. Manual review
samples a few percent of calls and calls that coverage; this product scores **all** of
them, from the same rubric, with the evidence attached.

Three audiences, not five. Broker and account teams read the Broker Scorecard, and quality
and operations staff read the same scorecards a supervisor does — but neither has a
distinct surface of its own, and drawing them as separate audiences implied a tailored
view that does not exist. They are readers of the Experience band, not audiences the
product is shaped around.

## 6. What is deliberately absent

Named here so their absence reads as a decision rather than an oversight:

- **Authentication and RBAC** (DEC-07) — a POC on localhost. The seam is at the API
  boundary and nothing is half-built.
- **Audio and ASR** (DEC-04) — the prototype's "upload audio" affordance was removed
  rather than left as dead UI.
- **A charting library** (A2) — charts are pure CSS and inline SVG.
- **The corpus batch run** (DEC-06) — built and working, but an *operator* tool rather
  than a product capability: it re-analyses the authored corpus to populate the
  dashboards. It is gated out of the navigation by `showCorpusRun` for exactly that
  reason, so it does not appear as a channel here.
- **A certification programme** — doc 10 §10 uses the HIPAA Security Rule as an organising
  frame for member-data exposure and explicitly declines to stand up a HIPAA or SOC 2
  audit effort. Conflating the two inflates scope and dilutes the controls that actually
  protect members.

## 7. Honest count

Across the diagram's twenty-six capabilities:

| Status | Count | Reading |
|---|---:|---|
| **LIVE** | 17 | The analysis engine and everything reading from it |
| **NEXT** | 3 | Redaction, provider gating, member-at-risk |
| **LATER** | 6 | The real-time lane, RAG, consent, and access control |

The pattern is worth stating plainly rather than leaving the reader to infer it: **the
intelligence is finished and the trust floor is not.** Seventeen live capabilities sit on
a governance layer that is one-third implemented. That is the correct shape for a POC
against a synthetic corpus, and the wrong shape for a pilot against real member calls.

## 8. The gate before real member data

One conclusion follows from the diagram's ordering, and it is the reason the trust band is
drawn as the floor.

Today `LLM_PROVIDER=openai`, `RedactionPort` resolves to `NoRedaction` with
`is_active = False`, and nothing in the code prevents a real transcript being pasted into
the Analyze screen. Against the synthetic corpus that is exactly what DEC-08 specifies.
The moment a real call is pasted, unredacted PHI is posted to a third party.

Three things close it, and they are independent of each other:

1. **Implement redaction** — the field list and the reversible-tokenisation approach are
   already specified in doc 10 §10. The port is called by the pipeline, so this is one
   adapter, not a restructure.
2. **Sign a BAA, or route through a cloud that already holds one** — Anthropic via
   Bedrock or Vertex, OpenAI via Azure. This is contractual work with a lead time, not
   engineering work.
3. **Make the code fail closed** — if the redactor is inactive, only local providers may
   be selected. `is_local()` already marks the seam. This one is small, and it holds even
   before either of the other two exists.

None of the fifteen LIVE capabilities changes when these land. That is the point of
drawing trust as a floor rather than a feature.

## 9. The context diagram

`14-product-context-diagram.svg` answers the three questions this one deliberately cannot,
in three panels. It is a *view* of document 10 rather than a second source of truth for it.

| Panel | Question | Source | The honest line on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Integration points** | What must it talk to? | Doc 10 §2 | Contact centre (media + CTI), M365 via Graph, knowledge repositories, Entra SSO, and an export-only feed to the lakehouse. **None are built** — the POC ingests pasted transcripts (DEC-04) |
| **Deployment options** | What shapes can it be run in? | Doc 10 §4 | POC (all local, ≈$0.5–1.2k/mo — *this build*), Hybrid (≈$2.4–3.1k/mo — *recommended*), Enterprise (≈$7–11k/mo). All three implement the same interfaces, so moving between them is configuration, not redesign |
| **Data lifecycle** | What is held, and for how long? | Doc 10 §10.1 | Audio 30–90 days, transcripts 2 years, analyses 7 years, aggregates indefinite, logs 1 year hot / 7 archive. **None enforced in this build** — the POC keeps everything, in one SQLite file |

The deployment panel is the one to reach for in the BAA and residency conversation in §8:
it shows, per option, where the models run and where member data sits. Option 1 needs no
BAA because nothing leaves the machine; Option 2 needs one for the deep lane only; Option
3 needs the full set.

## 10. Regenerating the diagrams

```bash
python scripts/make_product_diagram.py
python scripts/make_context_diagram.py
```

Text fit is asserted per label, so a caption that outgrows its box fails loudly rather
than silently overflowing. The script prints `no text-fit warnings` on a clean run.

Status marks live in the `BANDS`, `CH` and `PE` tables at the foot of that script — one
edit per capability, and the diagram, its legend and its dashed borders all follow. **Keep
them in step with the tables in §3**; the diagram and this document are the same claim
made twice, and the fastest way to lose a client's trust is for the two to disagree.
